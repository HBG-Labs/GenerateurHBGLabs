# Professional motion principles

These principles abstract design language; they do not encode any creator’s
style or reproduce an identifiable work.

1. **Motion causality** — one event visibly prepares, triggers, or reveals the
   next; motion is explanatory, not decoration.
2. **Transformation over replacement** — preserve identity, momentum, shape,
   scale, or color when two ideas are semantically related.
3. **Hierarchy and staging** — make the intended focus unmistakable; secondary
   motion supports rather than competes.
4. **Restraint** — a held or minimal composition can increase the force of the
   next impact. Premium does not mean constant movement.
5. **Overlap and follow-through** — coordinated secondary motion avoids the
   mechanical rhythm of fully sequential behaviors.
6. **Visual continuity** — anchors, persistent entities, camera momentum,
   motifs, and color fields connect scenes into a sequence rather than slides.
7. **Pacing contrast** — vary fast/slow, dense/sparse, small/large, and
   foreground/distant states across the full film.
8. **Asset coherence** — illustration, UI, texture, typography, and product
   parts must share tokens, material intent, and art direction.
9. **Negative space** — reserved regions are active composition tools, not empty
   failures.
10. **Purposeful camera** — camera reveals relationships or follows meaning; it
    is not a default zoom.
11. **Readable typography** — type may become geometry, mask, frame, or anchor,
    but HarfBuzz fitting and temporal readability remain authoritative.
12. **Designed cuts** — an impact, contrast, or rhythm cut can be professional;
    only accidental degradation to CUT is a failure.

## Technique composition examples

- `PERSISTENT_PRODUCT + CAMERA_FOLLOW + UI_CARD_EXTRACTION + SCALE_CONTINUITY`;
- `SUBJECT_ISOLATE + TYPE_BEHIND_SUBJECT + FOREGROUND_WIPE + POSTER_REASSEMBLE`;
- `PATH_DRAW + ARRIVAL_IMPACT + SHAPE_EXPANSION + MASK_REVEAL`;
- `TYPOGRAPHIC_HERO + TRACKING_BURST + WEIGHT_PULSE + WORD_TO_MASK`.

These are generic compositions. Their value depends on context, timing, assets,
and restraint; they are not templates.
